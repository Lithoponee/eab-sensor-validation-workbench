Question 01

Does more aptamer catch more target?

Two separate reasons, and they are worth keeping apart. The first is about capture: past a certain density the layer already catches everything diffusion delivers. The second is about signal: occupancy is a fraction, and a fraction has no density in it.

Why capture saturates so early

A molecule reaching the near-surface region can make repeated diffusive encounters before escaping. Consequently, capture may approach an absorbing-boundary limit before binding pockets occupy a large geometric area. The crossover shown here is a scaling estimate, not a universal density threshold.

Where this answer stops being true

Only at equilibrium, in an open cell. In a closed-chamber (limited-volume) read the per-cycle signal is Δf ≅ (H/Γ)(C − KDfinit): sensitivity falls as 1/Γ while speed rises as Γ, so density becomes a first-order design variable with a real optimum. See question 08. Say “at equilibrium, in an open cell” rather than flatly “no”.

Density can also change apparent affinity, but its sign is system-specific. Daniel 2013 found KDsurf = 3.14 − 0.30σ nM for a large, multivalent thrombin target: denser grafting produced tighter apparent binding through cooperative interactions. That sign must not be transferred to a small-molecule sensor; crowding can instead reduce folding and accessibility.

Question 02

Would faster flow help?

Transport and surface reaction act as resistances in series. Their ratio is the Damköhler number, and it decides whether a better flow cell can change anything at all.

1keff=1km+1konΓT,CsCb=11+Dam,k‾m=0.807(D2γ˙/L)1/3 Length-averaged Lévêque, γ̇ = 6U/h, developed laminar flow between parallel plates. Initial-rate result (θ ≪ 1).
Load-bearing assumption — the one that would break this argument

Cs/Cb = 1/(1+Dam) assumes a net flux. It is an initial-rate result. Set dθ/dt = 0 in the full two-compartment model and km cancels algebraically, leaving θ = Cfree/(KD+Cfree). At equilibrium nothing is being consumed, so there is no spatial gradient and Cs → Cb whatever the flow rate. In an open cell Cfree is maintained at the inlet concentration; in a sealed finite chamber it is found by conserving the total analyte. Transport dropping out of the equilibrium answer is not an assumption we made — it falls out of the general model.

In a passively supplied device, the source flux sets the exchange rate. A pump cannot be credited in a configuration whose sample supply cannot sustain that flow.

Question 03

How much actually binds

At equilibrium, a receptor responds to affinity and the free concentration at the surface. In an open reservoir that concentration is maintained and geometry drops out. In a sealed finite chamber, geometry re-enters through receptor capacity because binding lowers the free concentration.

the same thing, from one molecule's view

A receptor does not catch a target and keep it. Watch one for a long time: it spends most of its life empty, and occasionally holds a target for a while. Occupancy is just that duty cycle.

05.1

Is it even the right kind of sensor?

Before interpreting any gain, check what the current is coming from. A tethered reporter and a species diffusing in from solution obey different scan-rate laws, and the exponent is the cheapest diagnostic you have.

05.2

Frequency selects a timescale

The reporter is on a leash. It does not sit at one distance — it samples a whole distribution, many times, within a single pulse. Which electron-transfer rates the measurement is sensitive to is set by how long that pulse lasts.

05.3

Why a good fit is not evidence

Electron transfer falls exponentially with distance, so the tunnelling medium matters as much as the geometry. If analyte partitions into the monolayer it changes β — and that alone moves the current, with no conformational change at all.

kET(r)=k0e−βr,δkETkET=−rδβ β ≈ 1 Å⁻¹ for a saturated alkane chain. The reporter sits at r ≈ 10 Å.
kobs=k0⟨e−βr⟩,p(r)=𝒩(μ,s2)⇒kBkU=exp[−βΔμ+β22Δ(s2)] Δμequiv=β2Δ(s2) Average the exponential over the reporter-distance distribution—not the distance before exponentiating. Δμ = μB − μU and Δ(s²) = sB² − sU². The Gaussian closed form assumes the same β and k⁰ in both states and a distribution not strongly truncated by electrode contact.
The variance result: a testable resolution of the Å/nm mismatch

The mean-distance term is linear in β, whereas the variance term is quadratic. At β = 1 Å⁻¹, widening a Gaussian distribution from s = 3.0 to 3.2 Å is equivalent, in the averaged rate, to a 0.62 Å shift of its mean—even though the width changed by only 0.2 Å. That example gives a 1.86× rate factor; larger width changes are exponentially amplified without requiring the reporter's mean position to move by nanometres.

Broadening also adds probability to both the close, fast-transfer tail and the far, slow-transfer tail. When square-wave frequency selects different rate windows, that mechanism can produce opposite response signs. The reported +315% at 750 Hz and −82% at 20 Hz are therefore a validation target for the distribution model, not magnitudes predicted by the Gaussian equation alone. A quantitative prediction requires the measured or simulated pU(r) and pB(r), the pulse-response model, and checks that β and k⁰ remain state-independent.

Does the two-frequency trick actually help?

When electrochemical readout may be preferable to a FET

A field-effect readout senses charge only within a Debye length of the gate. The binding pocket does not sit on the surface — it sits several nanometres up, on a flexible tether.

Question 08

How fast a change can it follow?

Everything above asks how little. A continuous sensor also has to answer how fast, and that is a different calculation with different governing parameters. The sensor behaves as a low-pass filter: it reproduces slow changes, attenuates and phase-lags fast ones, and has a cutoff frequency computable before anything is built.

τD=H2D,τR=1konC+koff,τA=VQ1fc=∑i1fci The displayed τ_R equation is the maintained-concentration branch. In finite / sealed mode the calculation uses the exact closed-system linear relaxation time, τ_R = 1/[k_on√((R_T+C+K_D)²−4R_TC)]. f_c^D ≈ 0.65/τ_D, f_c^R ≈ 0.27/τ_R, f_c^A ≈ 0.39/τ_A. Cutoff is defined at 50% amplitude; the prefactors and series interpolation remain model-dependent.
The off-rate floor is a property of the valve, not the aptamer

It is standard to say that 1/koff is a floor on response time that no geometry beats. That holds only for an open cell, where the analyte is continuously resupplied, so the surface can relax only by letting complexes fall apart.

In the low-analyte limited-volume approximation, closing the chamber makes depletion part of the transient. The characteristic clock becomes τR,LV ≈ 1/(konCb,0) with Cb,0 = Γ/H. In that regime the approximate speed ratio α = Γ/(H·KD) increases as affinity tightens. Whether an immobilized device realizes this behavior must be checked against the full mass balance and experiment.

Lubken, Bergkamp, de Jong & Prins, ACS Sens. 2021, 6, 4471–4481.
The linearised speed–signal tradeoff

Within the low-analyte derivation, per-cycle sensitivity scales as H/Γ and the speed ratio as Γ/(H·KD), giving Δf · α ≈ C / KD. This invariant is useful for exploring the idealized tradeoff; departures are expected when occupancy is not small or the surface kinetics and sample exchange violate the model assumptions.

Two further caveats before using this mode: it has been demonstrated with binders free in solution rather than every possible immobilised construct, and a low passive supply flux can make the exchange phase dominate the cycle. Validate both the surface implementation and sample-replacement step experimentally.

Reducing uncertainty

Which measurement would most improve this scenario?

Target concentration is one high-impact uncertainty because it enters the affinity requirement directly. Surface KD, kon, gain, blank variability, and accessible probe density may be equally important depending on the selected parameter set.

Model and its limits. The sample-boundary selector determines the equilibrium model independently of flow: “maintained” fixes the reservoir concentration, while “finite / sealed” conserves the chamber inventory. Chamber inventory uses the cylindrical volume V = πrch²H, whereas the flow estimate treats the sensing region as a parallel-plate channel of width 2rwe; this mixed geometry is a deliberate reduced-order approximation, not a single exact flow-cell geometry. km uses a characteristic film estimate D/h without forced flow — a sealed transient chamber has no single steady km, so that branch is a scaling estimate, not a prediction — and the length-averaged Lévêque form 0.807(D²γ̇/L)1/3 with γ̇ = 6U/h under flow. Treat both as order-of-magnitude. Cs/Cb = 1/(1+Dam) holds only during unsaturated association; at true equilibrium the net flux vanishes and Cs = Cb. Assumes 1:1 reversible binding, a homogeneous accessible receptor population, and no non-specific adsorption.

On KD. The KD here is a thermodynamic constant. A value fitted from square-wave voltammetry is an apparent EC50 and may differ from it; immobilisation can shift it again. A measured surface affinity under the intended assay conditions gives the model a stronger basis than a solution-selection value.
