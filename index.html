Question 01

Does more aptamer catch more target?

Two separate reasons, and they are worth keeping apart. The first is about capture: past a certain density the layer already catches everything diffusion delivers. The second is about signal: occupancy is a fraction, and a fraction has no density in it.

Γ*=km4Drz,θeq=CsKD+Cs Γacc=faccΓ,d≈Γ−1/2,dacc≈(faccΓ)−1/2,ϕ=Γπ(dp/2)2 Γ* is the Berg–Purcell capture crossover. Γacc corrects the physical tether density by the independently estimated accessible fraction. Spacing and geometric coverage use the physical density and footprint diameter dₚ.
Why capture saturates so early

A molecule that lands on bare gold does not leave. It diffuses a few nanometres and returns, thousands of times over. A miss is not a loss — it is another attempt. So the layer behaves like a perfect absorber long before the pockets cover any meaningful area.

Berg & Purcell 1977 — a 1 µm cell needs ~3,000 receptors, 0.08% of its surface.
Independent check

SPR and BLI keep immobilisation density deliberately low, for exactly this reason: “Low flow rates and high immobilization levels can cause mass transport limitation.” The remedy they recommend — immobilise less, flow faster — is the one this model reaches independently.

Sartorius Octet applications guide, Figs 42–43
Where this answer stops being true

Only at equilibrium, in an open cell. In a closed-chamber (limited-volume) read the per-cycle signal is Δf ≅ (H/Γ)(C − KDfinit): sensitivity falls as 1/Γ while speed rises as Γ, so density becomes a first-order design variable with a real optimum. See question 07. Say “at equilibrium, in an open cell” rather than flatly “no”.

The real density effect points the other way: crowding measurably weakens surface affinity — KDsurf = 3.14 − 0.30σ nM (Daniel 2013). Denser is worse, not better.

Question 02

Would faster flow help?

Transport and surface reaction act as resistances in series. Their ratio is the Damköhler number, and it decides whether a better flow cell can change anything at all.

1keff=1km+1konΓT,CsCb=11+Dam,k‾m=0.807(D2γ˙/L)1/3 Length-averaged Lévêque, γ̇ = 6U/h, developed laminar flow between parallel plates. Initial-rate result (θ ≪ 1).
Load-bearing assumption — the one that would break this argument

Cs/Cb = 1/(1+Dam) assumes a net flux. It is an initial-rate result. Set dθ/dt = 0 in the full two-compartment model and km cancels algebraically, leaving θ = C/(KD+C). At equilibrium nothing is being consumed, so there is no gradient, and Cs → Cb whatever the flow rate. Transport dropping out of the equilibrium answer is not an assumption we made — it falls out of the general model. Quoting the depletion figure as a steady-state property of the reading is simply wrong.

In a passively supplied device, the source flux sets the exchange rate. A pump cannot be credited in a configuration whose sample supply cannot sustain that flow.

Question 04

How long it takes to get there

Below KD the association term collapses and kobs → koff, so response time stops depending on concentration. An affinity sensor consumes nothing once full, so depletion is a phase it passes through, never a state it sits in.

θ(t)=θeq[1−e−kobst],Leq=ΓmaxC+KD L_eq is the depth of solution that must be stripped to fill the surface — a tighter K_D needs a deeper strip.
Question 05

Does occupancy actually become current?

Read this before the rest of the section

Every other question on this page is computed. This one is not. The gain $G$ and the two currents IU(f), IB(f) are measured, never predicted — they depend on how the reporter's distance distribution reshapes on binding, which no model in this chain supplies. This is the single point where the forward calculation stops. Everything below shows what can be computed around that gap, and what it costs you to guess wrong about it.

And measuring G does not tell you what you measured. Write the tunnelling law with both variables free — ln(k′/k) = −(βΔr + rΔβ) — and one measured current carries two unknowns. A 1 Å move of the reporter and a 0.1 Å⁻¹ change in the tunnelling medium are the same observation. No amount of extra concentrations separates them; only a control does, and the decisive one is a scrambled sequence with the same backfill. If the scramble responds, you are measuring the monolayer, not binding.

I(f,t)=[1−θ]IU(f)+θIB(f),ΔII=θ·G The linear mixture. It assumes I_U and I_B are fixed — that neither depends on concentration. Question 05.3 is where that breaks.

05.1

Is it even the right kind of sensor?

Before interpreting any gain, check what the current is coming from. A tethered reporter and a species diffusing in from solution obey different scan-rate laws, and the exponent is the cheapest diagnostic you have.

05.2

Frequency selects a timescale

The reporter is on a leash. It does not sit at one distance — it samples a whole distribution, many times, within a single pulse. Which electron-transfer rates the measurement is sensitive to is set by how long that pulse lasts.

05.3

Why a good fit is not evidence

Electron transfer falls exponentially with distance, so the tunnelling medium matters as much as the geometry. If analyte partitions into the monolayer it changes β — and that alone moves the current, with no conformational change at all.

kET(r)=k0e−βr,δkETkET=−rδβ β ≈ 1 Å⁻¹ for a saturated alkane chain. The reporter sits at r ≈ 10 Å.
05.4

Does the two-frequency trick actually help?

KDM subtracts the relative response at two frequencies. Correlated drift cancels; uncorrelated noise adds in quadrature. Whether that is a net win depends on one number almost nobody reports.

Sdiff=S1−S2,Var=σ12+σ22−2ρσ1σ2⇒σdiffσ=2(1−ρ) Equal noise at both frequencies. The signal-only view is optimistic; the noise-only view is pessimistic. Both are drawn below.
05.5

Why this has to be electrochemical, not a FET

A field-effect readout senses charge only within a Debye length of the gate. The binding pocket does not sit on the surface — it sits several nanometres up, on a flexible tether.

Question 08

How fast a change can it follow?

Everything above asks how little. A continuous sensor also has to answer how fast, and that is a different calculation with different governing parameters. The sensor behaves as a low-pass filter: it reproduces slow changes, attenuates and phase-lags fast ones, and has a cutoff frequency computable before anything is built.

τD=H2D,τR=1konC+koff,τA=VQ1fc=∑i1fci f_c^D ≈ 0.65/τ_D, f_c^R ≈ 0.27/τ_R, f_c^A ≈ 0.39/τ_A. Cutoff defined at 50% amplitude — not 1/√2. Prefactors are specific to Lubken 2022's model and geometry. The series combination is our interpolation, not their published result.
The off-rate floor is a property of the valve, not the aptamer

It is standard to say that 1/koff is a floor on response time that no geometry beats. That holds only for an open cell, where the analyte is continuously resupplied, so the surface can relax only by letting complexes fall apart.

Close the chamber for the duration of the read and the analyte is consumed instead. Dissociation leaves the critical path entirely, and the clock becomes τR,LV = 1/(konCb,0) with Cb,0 = Γ/H — containing no KD and no koff. The speed-up is α = Γ/(H·KD), which grows as the binder tightens, exactly cancelling the usual penalty.

Lubken, Bergkamp, de Jong & Prins, ACS Sens. 2021, 6, 4471–4481.
And why we cannot use it yet — the cost is exact

Per-cycle sensitivity is H/Γ; the speed-up is Γ/(H·KD). Multiply and the geometry cancels: Δf · α = C / KD. Speed is bought from occupancy one for one. Chamber height and probe density only slide you along that line — only affinity moves the line.

Two further caveats before using this mode: it has been demonstrated with binders free in solution rather than every possible immobilised construct, and a low passive supply flux can make the exchange phase dominate the cycle. Validate both the surface implementation and sample-replacement step experimentally.

Measure the target concentration independently

A tenfold error in the assumed concentration changes the affinity requirement by the same factor. Establish the operating range with an independent analytical method before optimizing receptor affinity or device geometry.

Model and its limits. km uses a characteristic film estimate D/h in a static cell — a sealed transient chamber has no single steady km, so that branch is a scaling estimate, not a prediction — and the length-averaged Lévêque form 0.807(D²γ̇/L)1/3 with γ̇ = 6U/h under flow. Treat both as order-of-magnitude. Cs/Cb = 1/(1+Dam) holds only during unsaturated association; at true equilibrium the net flux vanishes and Cs = Cb regardless of flow. Occupancy in a static cell uses the closed-chamber mass balance, which conserves the finite target inventory; under flow it uses the maintained-concentration limit. Assumes 1:1 reversible binding, a homogeneous accessible receptor population, and no non-specific adsorption.

On KD. The KD here is a thermodynamic constant. A value fitted from square-wave voltammetry is an EC50 and can differ from it by orders of magnitude; immobilisation shifts it again. Feed this page a measured surface KD, not a SELEX number, before trusting any LOD it prints.
